#+title: The Illusion of Diminishing Returns: Measuring Long Horizon Execution in LLMs - Sinha, Akshit and Arun, Arvindh and Goel, Shashwat and Staab, Steffen and Geiping, Jonas

As LLM simple questions over and over again to observe what happens

- Non-diminishing benefits of scaling model size :: small improvement in per step compound to large improvement in long horizon
- Self-conditioning causes degradation in turn accuracy beyond long-context :: prior failures will cause higher error rate
- Unlike long-context, scaling model size does not mitigate self-conditioning :: big models still bad
- Thinking fixes self-conditioning :: models with thinking enabled no longer self-condition, even when the entire prior history has wrong answers, in contrast to non-thinking results.
- Without CoT, non-thinking models struggle to chain more than a few steps per turn.
